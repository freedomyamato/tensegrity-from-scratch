# Lesson 39: Assembly quality and inspection skills

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 09: Accessible teaching and workforce skills

## Objective

Demonstrate a practical quality-control task with traceable records.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 38; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 180 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

Quality work compares a component with a specified criterion. A classroom criterion must be explicit and achievable with the instrument. Sorting, labeling, reading instructions and documenting defects are transferable skills, but this activity does not establish occupational competence or promise employment. Use simulated defects instead of creating hazardous ones.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/learning-loop.svg)

## Worked example

A practice instruction requires a 140±2 mm span, measured with a 1 mm ruler. A 139 mm sample passes that stated classroom criterion; a 145 mm sample is held for review. These tolerances are educational choices, not engineering specifications.

## Materials and setup

Three labeled cord samples or drawings with 139,140 and 145 mm spans; ruler and inspection form.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Read the written span criterion aloud or show it visually.
2. Measure each sample and record the value.
3. Classify pass or hold without hiding borderline uncertainty.
4. Describe the defect and the required review or adjustment.
5. Have another person audit one record against the sample.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

The record identifies component, criterion, reading and action.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Do not confuse a classroom tolerance with an approved construction tolerance or accredited technician qualification.

## Teach-back quiz

1. Does 139 mm pass the example criterion?
2. What action applies to 145 mm?
3. Does this promise employment?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-09-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

Three inspection entries and one audited record.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
