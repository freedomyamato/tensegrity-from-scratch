# Lesson 37: Visual task sequences

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 09: Accessible teaching and workforce skills

## Objective

Produce a predictable assembly sequence with observable checks.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 36; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 180 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

A visual sequence reduces ambiguity by showing the part, action and completion cue. Color should be supported by numbers or shapes because color perception and printing differ. Use one action per card, allow a learner to pause, and show what a correct endpoint connection looks like. The method is an educational option, not a universal intervention.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/learning-loop.svg)

## Worked example

Card: “Find S0; connect nodes 0 and 4; check both node numbers; place the finished card in DONE.” This is more precise than showing an unlabeled photograph of a partly built prism.

## Materials and setup

Paper cards, labeled components and templates/task-card.md.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Choose one short assembly segment with three or four actions.
2. Make a card for each action with labels and completion cue.
3. Include a help request and pause option.
4. Try the cards with a willing learner or peer using their preferred supports.
5. Record where the sequence was unclear and revise one card.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

The learner can identify what comes next and when a step is finished.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Do not remove helpful support just to score independence. Avoid assumptions about autism based on diagnosis alone.

## Teach-back quiz

1. Why combine color with labels?
2. What is a completion cue?
3. Should every learner use the same support?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-09-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

A tested short task-card sequence.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
