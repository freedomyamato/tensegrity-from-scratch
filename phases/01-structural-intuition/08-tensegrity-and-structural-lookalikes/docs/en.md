# Lesson 08: Tensegrity and structural lookalikes

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 01: Structural intuition

## Objective

Classify structures by connectivity and member roles rather than appearance.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 07; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 180 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

The classic tensegrity picture uses separated compression members coupled by a continuous tension network. Mathematical tensegrity frameworks also include broader cable/strut/bar definitions. A floating-platform display can be an excellent statics example while differing from the classic isolated-strut arrangement. State the definition you are using.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/prism.svg)

## Worked example

A platform hung from a central cable and stabilized by side ties is not automatically the same topology as a three-strut prism. Both may contain tension and compression, but their supports and connected compression paths differ.

## Materials and setup

Supplied prism drawing, a sketch of a suspended platform, pencil.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Mark every node and member in both structures.
2. Label cables, struts and any rigid connected frames.
3. Trace the compression connectivity and external supports.
4. Write a classification with the definition stated first.
5. Explain both designs without describing either as magic or antigravity.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

The comparison remains accurate even when marketing names are inconsistent.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

A structure does not float without load transfer. Do not treat terminology debates as substitutes for force diagrams.

## Teach-back quiz

1. What should classification state first?
2. Does “floating” mean no load path?
3. Can a lookalike still teach statics?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-01-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

A comparison table of nodes, members, supports and definitions.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
