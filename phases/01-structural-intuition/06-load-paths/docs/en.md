# Lesson 06: Load paths

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 01: Structural intuition

## Objective

Trace a load through connections to external supports.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 05; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 180 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

A load path follows how forces transfer between members and eventually to supports. Internal forces occur in action–reaction pairs; reactions from the table or anchors are external to the structure. A complete diagram must say which object is being isolated. A free prism with only self-stress is a different problem from a prism resting on a table under gravity.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/prism.svg)

## Worked example

For a tabletop model with total mass 0.2 kg, its weight is approximately 1.962 N using g=9.81 m/s². In static vertical balance the sum of upward table reactions is 1.962 N. Equal individual reactions require additional symmetry assumptions.

## Materials and setup

A model or the supplied prism drawing; paper and colored pencils.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Draw a boundary around the entire model.
2. Mark gravity and table contact forces outside that boundary.
3. Choose one top node and draw its connected members.
4. Trace the possible force routes to bottom contacts.
5. Explain why the free self-stress model has no table reactions but the physical model does.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

The whole-structure diagram includes external forces while the node diagram contains member forces.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Do not distribute weight equally without checking symmetry, contact and center of mass.

## Teach-back quiz

1. Where does a tabletop load ultimately go?
2. Are table reactions internal?
3. What is the weight of 0.2 kg?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-01-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

Whole-model and single-node load-path sketches.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
