# Lesson 36: AI-assisted analysis and inspection

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 08: Sensors, controls and AI

## Objective

Use AI for explanation while retaining independent checks and evidence.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 35; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 240 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

AI can suggest calculations, summarize notes and identify possible defects in images. It can also invent measurements, misunderstand topology or miss a defect. Treat outputs as proposals: verify units, endpoints, equations and facts against evidence. Ground every completion decision in work the learner actually supplies.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/learning-loop.svg)

## Worked example

An AI says a 140 mm member is 1.4 m and produces plausible forces. A unit check catches the factor-of-ten error before interpreting the result. An AI photo comment “joint looks secure” cannot establish pullout strength or hidden damage.

## Materials and setup

A completed experiment record, optional AI assistant; no external service is required.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Give an assistant a non-identifying member table or use the supplied faulty-example scenario.
2. Ask it to explain the load path and list assumptions.
3. Independently verify one unit conversion and one force calculation.
4. Classify each statement as observed, calculated, inferred or unsupported.
5. Revise the report while retaining disagreements and missing evidence.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

Useful explanation survives checks; unsupported confidence is removed.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Do not upload personal learner records or rely on image recognition for structural approval.

## Teach-back quiz

1. What is the correct conversion of 140 mm?
2. Can a photo prove joint capacity?
3. Who provides experimental evidence?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-08-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

An AI-output review with independent corrections.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
