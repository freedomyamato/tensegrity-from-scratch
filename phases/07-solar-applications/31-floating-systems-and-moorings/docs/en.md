# Lesson 31: Floating systems and moorings

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 07: Solar and Singapore applications

## Objective

Explain buoyancy and station-keeping as additional systems beyond tensegrity.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 30; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 270 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

A floating solar concept needs buoyancy, stability, moorings, electrical protection and maintenance. Tensegrity can be a support geometry but does not supply flotation by itself. For static buoyancy, displaced fluid weight balances total weight. Moorings manage movement and environmental loads; they are not represented by a free self-stress model.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/solar-measurement.svg)

## Worked example

A 1 kg assembly floating in fresh water of density 1000 kg/m³ displaces about 0.001 m³, or one liter, at balance. Practical flotation needs allowance for reserve buoyancy and stability; one liter of nominal displacement is not a complete float design.

## Materials and setup

Paper concept, calculator and a fictional pond plan. Water testing is not required.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Calculate displacement for illustrative total masses 1 and 2 kg.
2. Draw floats, support geometry, mooring points and electrical route as separate systems.
3. Consider tilt, water-level changes, debris and access for repair.
4. List conditions not captured by the tabletop prism.
5. Prepare a basin-test proposal for qualified review rather than placing equipment on open water.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

The diagram separates structure from flotation and station-keeping.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Do not place electrical prototypes on public water or work near water without a suitable supervised plan.

## Teach-back quiz

1. Does tensegrity provide buoyancy automatically?
2. How much water does 1 kg displace in the example?
3. What do moorings control?
