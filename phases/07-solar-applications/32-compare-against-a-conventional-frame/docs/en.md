# Lesson 32: Compare against a conventional frame

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 07: Solar and Singapore applications

## Objective

Design a fair baseline comparison and accept tradeoffs.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 31; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 270 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

A comparison needs a common task and comparable boundary conditions. Match panel area, load, height/angle and available footprint where possible. Record mass, cost, assembly time, deflection and maintenance access. If two objectives conflict, report them separately rather than hiding them in an arbitrary total score.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/solar-measurement.svg)

## Worked example

Synthetic results: tensegrity mass 120 g and assembly 25 minutes; frame mass 180 g and assembly 8 minutes. The tensegrity example is lighter but slower to build. These numbers are teaching data, not evidence of a general advantage.

## Materials and setup

Two tabletop dummy-panel supports or illustrative data; templates/comparison.csv.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Define the same task for both concepts.
2. Choose three metrics and measurement methods before observing results.
3. Record unmatched dimensions or materials as confounders.
4. Measure or clearly label synthetic values, retaining repeat trials.
5. Write a recommendation tied to the intended user and maintenance conditions.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

The recommendation states what improved, what worsened and how certain the result is.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Avoid comparing a delicate miniature with an overbuilt frame and presenting the mass difference as universal superiority.

## Teach-back quiz

1. What should be fixed before testing?
2. What is a confounder?
3. Can a negative result be useful?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-07-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

A baseline comparison with tradeoffs and limitations.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
