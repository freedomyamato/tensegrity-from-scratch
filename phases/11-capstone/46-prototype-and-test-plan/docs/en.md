# Lesson 46: Prototype and test plan

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 11: Capstone and research judgment

## Objective

Prepare comparable trials before observing the result.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 45; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 240 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

A test plan identifies specimens, conditions, instruments, sequence and stopping rules. Repeats help reveal variation but do not erase bias. Alternate or randomize order where conditions may drift. Physical tests use only suitable low-energy tabletop handling, while structural capacity and public-use claims require separate review.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/learning-loop.svg)

## Worked example

For rebuild repeatability, use three rebuilds of each support and measure angle with the same method. Alternate A,B,A,B,A,B. Record all attempts, including failures. Define handling, joint inspection and how to treat an unreadable angle before beginning.

## Materials and setup

Two suitable tabletop supports or simulation-only capstone; comparison and experiment templates.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Assign specimen and build IDs.
2. Set common dimensions/load conditions and list unmatched factors.
3. Choose three repeat trials and an alternating sequence.
4. Specify instruments, resolution and stop conditions.
5. Have a peer check that the plan could be repeated, then perform only the bounded trial.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

Raw records correspond to the planned sequence and do not quietly omit unsuccessful attempts.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Do not increase loads after seeing encouraging results. A stopped trial is evidence to retain.

## Teach-back quiz

1. Why alternate order?
2. Should failed trials be removed?
3. When are acceptance criteria chosen?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-11-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

A reviewed test plan and complete trial record.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
