# Lesson 47: Results and limitations

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 11: Capstone and research judgment

## Objective

Summarize evidence without overstating precision or generality.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 46; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 240 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

Report raw values, a summary and the boundary of your claim. A mean alone hides spread. Explain confounders, stopped trials and measurement limits. Separate numerical verification from physical validation. For an exploratory hypothesis, specify how future observations might falsify it rather than interpreting every result as support.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/learning-loop.svg)

## Worked example

Synthetic angle errors A=(1,2,3)° and B=(1,1,2)° have means 2° and 1.33° with ranges 2° and 1°. The sample suggests B is more repeatable here, but three trials and instrument resolution do not establish a universal structural advantage.

## Materials and setup

Trial records, calculator and templates/capstone-report.md.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Present raw values including stopped or missing trials.
2. Calculate mean and range for each comparable metric.
3. Describe instrument resolution and uncontrolled differences.
4. State a conclusion restricted to tested specimens and conditions.
5. Name one next experiment that would reduce an important uncertainty.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

The report supports a narrow conclusion and an explicit next step.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Do not claim statistical significance from these small example summaries or present synthetic values as measurements.

## Teach-back quiz

1. What can a mean hide?
2. What should limit a conclusion?
3. What is A’s range?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-11-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

A results table, limitations section and next-test proposal.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
