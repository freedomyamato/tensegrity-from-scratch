# Lesson 13: Units and vectors

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 03: Statics and useful mathematics

## Objective

Convert dimensions to SI and compute a member direction.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 12; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 270 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

A vector has components and direction. For endpoints a and b, d=b−a; length is sqrt(dx²+dy²+dz²); unit direction is d/length. Mass in kg and force in N differ by acceleration: weight W=mg. Unit conversion must precede substitution into SI equations.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/prism.svg)

## Worked example

For a=(0,0,0) m and b=(0.03,0.04,0) m, d=(0.03,0.04,0), length=0.05 m and unit direction=(0.6,0.8,0). A 2 N tension acting from a toward b has components (1.2,1.6,0) N.

## Materials and setup

Calculator, paper; optional Python console.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Convert 80,140 and 200 mm to m.
2. Compute the worked example length and unit vector.
3. Check that the unit-vector squared components sum to one.
4. Convert 100 g to kg and calculate its weight using g=9.81.
5. Draw the vector and annotate units on every number.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

The example force components combine to magnitude 2 N.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Do not enter mm into an equation expecting m. A zero-length member has no defined unit direction.

## Teach-back quiz

1. What is 140 mm in m?
2. What is the example unit vector?
3. What is 100 g’s weight?
