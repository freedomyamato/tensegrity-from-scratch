# Lesson 14: Free-body diagrams

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 03: Statics and useful mathematics

## Objective

Draw a selected object with all external forces and no hidden support assumptions.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 13; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 270 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

Choose the body before drawing forces. A cable pulls the isolated node toward its other endpoint. A compressed strut pushes the isolated node away from its other endpoint. Draw reactions only where a support acts on the chosen body. Internal members disappear when isolating the entire structure, but their forces appear when isolating one node.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/prism.svg)

## Worked example

For a node connected to a horizontal cable on its right, that cable contributes a rightward force. A compressed strut ending above the node pushes away from its far endpoint, with a downward component. Gravity and a support reaction must be included when present.

## Materials and setup

Paper, ruler and prism topology drawing.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Draw boundaries for one member, one node and the whole model.
2. At node 0, draw the four member directions using the supplied coordinates.
3. Assign tensile arrows to cables and compressive arrows to the strut.
4. Add external forces only for the physical supported case.
5. Check whether any reaction has been assumed without a real support.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

Different isolated bodies legitimately have different external-force sets.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Do not copy the whole-structure reaction into every node diagram. Each support acts at a particular contact.

## Teach-back quiz

1. Where does cable tension point on a node?
2. Do internal member forces remain on the whole-body diagram?
3. Why define a boundary?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-03-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

Three free-body diagrams with a written sign convention.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
