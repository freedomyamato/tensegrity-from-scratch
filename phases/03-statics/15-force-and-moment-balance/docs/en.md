# Lesson 15: Force and moment balance

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 03: Statics and useful mathematics

## Objective

Solve a simple supported load and understand why force balance is insufficient alone.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 14; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 270 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

For a static rigid body, summed forces and summed moments must vanish. Moment depends on force and perpendicular lever arm. A simple two-support example isolates the principle before using it in complicated geometry. This calculation does not establish joint capacity or geometric stability.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/prism.svg)

## Worked example

A 2 N downward load acts 0.15 m from the left support of a 0.30 m beam. Moments about the left support give Rright×0.30−2×0.15=0, hence Rright=1 N. Vertical balance gives Rleft=1 N. Moving the load to 0.10 m gives Rright=0.667 N and Rleft=1.333 N.

## Materials and setup

Paper, calculator and ruler; use sketches rather than adding a real beam load.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Draw the two-support system and label distances.
2. Solve both load positions and check vertical force totals.
3. Verify moments about the right support as an independent check.
4. Explain which symmetry assumption justified equal reactions in the first case.
5. Write why these equations do not certify a tensegrity prism.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

Reaction distribution changes when the load position changes.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

A force outside the support span may require uplift resistance; do not assume contacts can pull.

## Teach-back quiz

1. What is moment’s SI unit?
2. Why are the first reactions equal?
3. Is force balance alone enough for a rigid-body statics check?
