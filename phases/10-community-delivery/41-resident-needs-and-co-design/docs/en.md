# Lesson 41: Resident needs and co-design

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 10: Community design and delivery

## Objective

Convert a community idea into requirements without inventing resident support.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 40; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 180 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

Co-design starts with users, constraints and ownership. A solar sculpture may be valued for shade, identity, learning or energy, and these goals can conflict. Separate actual feedback from assumptions. Use fictional stakeholder scenarios until real participation is arranged with consent; do not present simulated quotes as a survey.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/learning-loop.svg)

## Worked example

Fictional resident A values shade; maintenance staff need unobstructed access; resident B wants a familiar estate symbol. A design that meets the symbol request but blocks access fails an important requirement. Energy output alone cannot settle that conflict.

## Materials and setup

Fictional stakeholder cards and templates/community-brief.md.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. List three stakeholder roles and a plausible need for each.
2. Mark each need as assumption or actual feedback.
3. Translate needs into observable requirements, such as an unobstructed route.
4. Identify conflicts and one question to resolve each.
5. Propose a small tabletop co-design session with participation options.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

The brief distinguishes stakeholder evidence from designer assumptions.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Do not imply endorsement by HDB, NAC, Pathlight or residents when none was obtained.

## Teach-back quiz

1. What separates evidence from assumption?
2. Can user needs conflict?
3. What is a useful next step?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-10-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

A stakeholder/requirements table with evidence status.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
