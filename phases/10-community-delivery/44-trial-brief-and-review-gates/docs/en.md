# Lesson 44: Trial brief and review gates

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 10: Community design and delivery

## Objective

Define a bounded trial with evidence-based go/no-go decisions.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 43; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 180 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

A trial is a controlled way to answer a question. It has scope, responsible people, stop conditions and an evidence requirement for progression. An indoor tabletop demonstration is a different stage from a public installation. Gates should make that distinction explicit instead of turning enthusiasm into assumed approval.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/learning-loop.svg)

## Worked example

Gate 1: tabletop model has secure joints and repeatable assembly. Gate 2: independent geometry/force checks documented. Gate 3: any full-scale proposal receives qualified site-specific review. Passing gates 1–2 does not automatically pass gate 3.

## Materials and setup

Templates/site-review.md and completed concept brief.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Define one tabletop trial question and its limit.
2. Assign who facilitates and who checks components.
3. Write two measurable acceptance criteria and stop conditions.
4. List evidence needed before a larger or public stage.
5. Make a go/no-go decision from the available record, marking missing evidence.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

The decision can be reviewed and may correctly defer a larger stage.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Do not obtain “approval” by relabeling an unreviewed installation as a prototype.

## Teach-back quiz

1. What distinguishes stages?
2. What makes a gate reviewable?
3. Does passing tabletop tests approve public installation?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-10-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

A bounded trial brief with gate decisions.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
