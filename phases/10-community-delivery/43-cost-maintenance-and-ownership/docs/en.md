# Lesson 43: Cost, maintenance and ownership

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 10: Community design and delivery

## Objective

Estimate lifecycle responsibilities rather than purchase price alone.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 42; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 180 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

Costs include materials, assembly labor, inspections, replacements, access and end-of-life work. Use quantities and unit costs with source/date or explicitly assumed values. Ownership names who maintains records, decides repairs and pays. A volunteer project still consumes time and materials; donated work should not disappear from the estimate.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/learning-loop.svg)

## Worked example

Illustrative values: materials S$30, two assembly hours at an assumed S$15/hour and an assumed annual inspection cost S$10 produce S$60 initial resource cost and S$70 including one year of inspection. These are planning examples, not market prices.

## Materials and setup

Bill-of-materials template, paper calculator and maintenance plan.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Enter quantities and actual or explicitly assumed unit costs.
2. Add assembly hours separately from materials.
3. Name likely replacement components and inspection work.
4. Assign an owner, maintainer and repair decision-maker.
5. Compare initial and lifecycle estimates with the baseline frame.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

The estimate reveals assumptions and recurring work, not just component totals.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Do not claim current Singapore prices without checking them. Never imply an organization has accepted ownership.

## Teach-back quiz

1. Why count donated hours?
2. What must a cost source include?
3. Who decides a repair?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-10-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

A cost estimate and responsibility matrix.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
