# Lesson 42: Sculpture identity and alternatives

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 10: Community design and delivery

## Objective

Develop distinct concepts and compare them against requirements.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 41; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 180 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

Alternatives should differ in a meaningful design choice, not just color. Compare a tensegrity sculpture, conventional canopy concept and educational display against the same requirements. Aesthetic preference can be recorded alongside maintenance, access and performance evidence. “One estate, one solar sculpture” is a design vision, not a requirement to use the same structure everywhere.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/learning-loop.svg)

## Worked example

Concept A emphasizes a compact educational prism; B a conventional support with local artwork; C an adjustable tabletop sculpture. A display-only site may prefer A, while a maintenance-limited setting may prefer B. Selection follows the task and evidence.

## Materials and setup

Paper, stakeholder brief and a concept comparison table.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Sketch three concepts with different structural or use choices.
2. Annotate panel/dummy-panel area, public interaction and access.
3. Compare each with three requirements from lesson 41.
4. Mark feasibility questions rather than inventing answers.
5. Choose one for the next prototype and state why.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

The selected concept has a defensible connection to the intended user and task.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Do not disguise an untested architectural rendering as an approved or buildable design.

## Teach-back quiz

1. What makes alternatives meaningful?
2. Can aesthetics alone prove feasibility?
3. Must every estate use the same design?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-10-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

Three concept sketches and selection reasoning.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
