# Lesson 10: Build a three-strut prism

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 02: Geometry and making

## Objective

Assemble a low-tension classroom model using a defined topology.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 09; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 270 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

A jig supports nodes while cables are connected. Begin with adjustable loops and small prestress rather than forcing fixed cut lengths. The target ideal geometry has radius 80 mm, height 140 mm and a 30° top-triangle rotation. Joints and material compliance mean the physical model will differ from the point-node calculation.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/prism.svg)

## Worked example

Ideal member spans are about 138.56 mm for triangle edges, 146.00 mm for cross cables and 208.53 mm for struts. Use the generated member table for exact values. Knot allowances and the node-center definition are additional; the values are not universal cut lengths.

## Materials and setup

Three blunt lightweight struts, nine adjustable cord segments, soft secure attachment loops, tray, optional cardboard positioning jig; see the build guide.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Read builds/three-strut-prism.md completely and inspect your joint samples.
2. Mark bottom node positions and rotated top positions on the jig.
3. Place struts along 0–4, 1–5 and 2–3 while the jig supports them.
4. Attach bottom and top triangle cables, then C0,C1,C2; snug gradually in small symmetric increments.
5. Remove support only if joints hold; record all node spans and photograph the model.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

A connected tabletop model should hold a recognizable prism shape if joints and prestress are suitable; physical success is not guaranteed by the ideal calculation.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

If it collapses, return it to the jig and check node order, loose joints and unequal spans. Do not cure collapse by adding high tension.

## Teach-back quiz

1. Why use adjustable loops first?
2. Which strut connects node 0?
3. Does this repository certify the physical build?

## Evidence to submit

A measured model or a documented unsuccessful attempt and diagnosis.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
