# Lesson 12: Repeatable assembly and joints

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 02: Geometry and making

## Objective

Turn a successful assembly into instructions another person can follow.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 11; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 270 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

Repeatability depends on component identification, joint consistency and inspection points. A procedure should identify an observable check after each action. Photographs help, but labels and measured spans carry the information. Record assembly time separately from time spent learning the method.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/prism.svg)

## Worked example

“Attach S0 to nodes 0 and 4, then check both labels” is reproducible. “Connect the long rod here” is ambiguous when three rods look alike. If the two builds have heights 140 and 148 mm, inspect cable settings and endpoint definitions before claiming equivalence.

## Materials and setup

Labeled kit, ruler, timer, sample joint and private photo record.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Write a numbered assembly procedure with one check per step.
2. Add node labels and a joint close-up.
3. Disassemble only when the low-tension model can be released safely.
4. Rebuild using the procedure, or ask a partner to use it.
5. Compare three dimensions, time and any interpretation questions.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

The second build exposes missing instructions and produces a measurable comparison.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

A fast build with unsecured joints is not a successful repeat. Avoid measuring only the best attempt.

## Teach-back quiz

1. What makes a step unambiguous?
2. Why compare dimensions?
3. Should questions during assembly be recorded?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-02-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

Revision 2 assembly instructions and repeatability table.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
