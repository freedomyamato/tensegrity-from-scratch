# Lesson 02: Materials and tools

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 00: Orientation and workshop setup

## Objective

Prepare a small kit and distinguish geometric dimensions from connection allowances.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 01; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 90 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

A model includes members and joints. An ideal node is a point; a real knot, loop or cap occupies space and may slip. Record both the distance between node centers and the total cut length. Start with blunt, lightweight struts, cord and adjustable loops; use elastic only as a clearly documented classroom substitution.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/learning-loop.svg)

## Worked example

A target cable has a 140 mm node-to-node span. Two 15 mm knot allowances suggest an initial 170 mm cut, but actual knot take-up must be checked on a sample. Never treat 170 mm as the structural member length.

## Materials and setup

Blunt craft struts or a classroom kit, cord, ruler, reusable clips, tape, tray and optional pre-cut loops. Cutting is performed by a competent adult when needed.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Inventory each item and state its role.
2. Inspect strut ends and remove sharp or damaged parts.
3. Make one low-tension sample joint; mark its start position.
4. Measure its node span, overall length and repeatability.
5. Write a bill of materials using templates/bill-of-materials.csv.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

The materials list includes joint details, not only rods and strings.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Avoid thin wire, strong elastic, large suspended loads and improvised powered tools in introductory work.

## Teach-back quiz

1. Why is cut length different from node span?
2. What should an inspection reject?
3. Why record elastic-band use?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-00-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

A materials list and a photographed, measured sample joint.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
