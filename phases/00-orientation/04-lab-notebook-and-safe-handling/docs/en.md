# Lesson 04: Lab notebook and safe handling

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 00: Orientation and workshop setup

## Objective

Make a traceable experiment record and define a stop condition.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 03; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 90 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

A notebook separates prediction from observation. Record the setup before testing, then the result, then your interpretation. A revised design receives a new version identifier; do not overwrite the first result. Stop conditions are observable events such as joint slip or a damaged strut, not vague requests to “be careful.”

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/learning-loop.svg)

## Worked example

Prediction: shortening C0 will tilt the prism. Observation: C0 was shortened approximately 2 mm and the top moved sideways, but the angle was not measured. Interpretation: the result suggests asymmetry; it does not establish a quantitative tilt law.

## Materials and setup

Notebook template, pencil, tray and a low-tension sample joint.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Copy templates/experiment.md into a private local folder.
2. Assign a specimen ID and write your prediction.
3. Record dimensions, boundary conditions, tools and one stop condition.
4. Gently repeat the sample-joint handling test from lesson 02.
5. Log what occurred, including missing measurements; choose one next action.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

A reader can distinguish the expected result, observed fact and explanation.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Keep faces away from stretched components. Stop on slip, fraying or unexpected motion. Do not add learner-identifying photographs to a public repository.

## Teach-back quiz

1. When is the prediction written?
2. What distinguishes observation from inference?
3. Name a stop condition.

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-00-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

A completed experiment record with a version ID and stop condition.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
