# Lesson 27: Wind and changing loads

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 06: Dynamics and resilience

## Objective

Explore wind-load scaling and identify missing full-scale information.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 26; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 240 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

A simplified drag estimate is F=½ρV²CdA. Density ρ is kg/m³, speed V is m/s, coefficient Cd dimensionless and area A m². Real wind loading includes pressure distribution, uplift, gusts, direction, shielding and dynamic response. This lesson explores scaling; it does not supply Singapore design wind loads.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/learning-loop.svg)

## Worked example

For illustrative ρ=1.2, Cd=1, A=0.1 m² and V=5 m/s, F=1.5 N. At 10 m/s it is 6 N. Doubling speed quadruples the simplified force. Cd=1 is an assumed example, not a measured panel coefficient.

## Materials and setup

Calculator and a dummy-panel sketch; no wind machine required.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Calculate force at 5,10 and 15 m/s using the stated assumptions.
2. Change area by a factor of two and calculate the effect.
3. Draw force directions for uplift and lateral loading.
4. List missing site and aerodynamic inputs for a rooftop canopy.
5. Write a professional-review question instead of claiming the prototype is storm-proof.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

Quadratic speed dependence is apparent and missing assumptions are explicit.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

A desk-fan trial cannot be extrapolated to severe storms without similarity and load analysis.

## Teach-back quiz

1. How does force scale with speed here?
2. Is Cd=1 universal?
3. Does this give approved design loads?
