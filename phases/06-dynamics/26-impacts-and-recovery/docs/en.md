# Lesson 26: Impacts and recovery

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 06: Dynamics and resilience

## Objective

Define a repeatable low-energy disturbance and measure recovery.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 25; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 240 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

Resilience must be tied to a specific disturbance and observable response. Returning to a similar shape does not establish lack of damage. Impact energy depends on mass and velocity, or mgh for an ideal drop. Our classroom exercise uses a small controlled displacement and release rather than dropping a structure.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/learning-loop.svg)

## Worked example

For a linear k=4 N/m spring displaced 0.01 m, stored energy is ½kx²=0.0002 J. Doubling displacement quadruples this ideal energy. A real model can have slack cables, friction and several modes, so the equation is only an illustrative baseline.

## Materials and setup

Low-tension tabletop model, ruler, tray, timer or camera; alternative: oscillator lab.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Record initial geometry and inspect joints.
2. Set a very small displacement that stays within gentle handling; record it.
3. Release without pushing and record settling behavior.
4. Repeat three times after inspection, stopping on slip or damage.
5. Compare final span and joint witness marks with the initial record.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

Results separate motion recovery from geometry retention and connection condition.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Do not drop the model or claim shock resistance from a hand disturbance. If release is unpredictable, use simulation instead.

## Teach-back quiz

1. What happens to ideal energy if displacement doubles?
2. Does shape recovery prove no damage?
3. What should be inspected after a trial?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-06-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

A disturbance protocol and three-trial recovery table.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
