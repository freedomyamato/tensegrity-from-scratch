# Lesson 19: Joints, slip and creep

[Course contents](../../../../COURSE.md) · Phase 04: Materials and failure

## Objective

Separate immediate joint movement from time-dependent deformation.

**Prerequisite:** Lesson 18; or demonstrate its evidence check.

**Planning time:** approximately 240 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

## Concept

Slip changes the attachment position; creep is time-dependent deformation under sustained load. Both change geometry and prestress. A witness mark across a joint helps identify slip, while a span measurement over time captures total movement. More than one cause can act at once, so observations should not force a single diagnosis.

![Supporting diagram for this phase](../../../../assets/prism.svg)

## Worked example

A span changes from 140 to 142 mm over ten minutes. If the witness mark also moves 2 mm, joint slip is plausible. If the mark stays fixed, cord or elastic creep remains plausible, along with measurement error. One measurement alone cannot distinguish them.

## Materials and setup

Low-tension sample connection, tape witness mark, ruler and timer; no added hanging masses.

Use a private copy of the [experiment record](../../../../templates/experiment.md). Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

## Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

1. Mark both sides of one attachment and record initial span.
2. Keep a small, unchanged initial tension and note the method.
3. Read span and marks at 0,2,5 and 10 minutes.
4. Release tension gently and record whether length recovers.
5. Compare the observations with competing explanations.

## Expected behavior and interpretation

The time record can reveal motion at the joint, material deformation or inconclusive change.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

## Troubleshooting and limits

Keep ambient conditions and measuring method consistent. Stop if movement becomes sudden.

## Teach-back quiz

1. What does a witness mark reveal?
2. What distinguishes creep from instant elastic extension?
3. Can a 2 mm change alone identify the cause?

[Facilitator answer key](../../../../assessments/phase-04-answers.md) — attempt the questions first.

## Evidence to submit

A span-versus-time record and diagnosis with uncertainty.

Include your prediction, setup, results with units or criteria, and one limitation. Use the [rubric](../../../../assessments/RUBRIC.md): demonstrate each applicable dimension at level 2 or above before progressing. Numerical examples count as calculation evidence; they are not physical test results.

## Facilitator adaptation

Offer a sketch, demonstration or pointing response as alternatives to speech. Provide pre-labeled parts, shorter sessions or a quiet workspace if chosen by the learner. Preserve the objective while adapting unnecessary task demands.
