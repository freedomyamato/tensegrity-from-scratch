# Optional module: Quantum Tensegrity as a research hypothesis

Prerequisites: the classical mechanics/digital-model phases, linear algebra and independent study of quantum mechanics. Planning allowance: an additional eight hours for a research brief, not included in the 180-hour core. This module does not present a new validated physical theory.

## Distinguish three possible projects

| Project type | Meaning | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Structural analogy | Compare network features with other mathematical systems | A physical quantum mechanism |
| Quantum network model | Specify quantum degrees of freedom and their dynamics | A relationship to spacetime/gravity without further derivation |
| Laboratory quantum simulation | Specify a realizable quantum system and measurements | Validation merely from a classical craft model |

Classical cable equilibrium and quantum entanglement are different concepts. A graph shared by two models does not make their physical meanings equivalent. Quantum correlations cannot be inferred from mechanical tension alone.

## Task 1: classical baseline

Describe the prism topology, coordinates, force-density matrix and equilibrium checks. State what stability analysis is missing. Reproduce one numerical result independently. This creates a precise classical reference instead of a metaphor.

## Task 2: identify any proposed quantum content

Name the degrees of freedom, state space and observables. If proposing a quantum model, define a Hamiltonian or other explicit dynamics with units and assumptions. Explain whether “tension” is a physical force, a coupling parameter or only an analogy. Do not interchange these meanings mid-argument.

## Task 3: comparison and falsifiability

Find primary literature appropriate to the actual proposed model. Make a claim table: existing result, proposed extension, prediction and possible rejection evidence. Distinguish a prediction derived from equations from a hoped-for interpretation. A claim about entanglement needs suitable quantum observables; a claim about gravity needs an explicit relation to existing gravitational models and testable implications.

## Worked conceptual example

Hypothesis: changing classical network connectivity changes its vibration spectrum. This can be tested with an appropriate classical dynamic model or experiment. It may be interesting, but it does not establish a quantum or gravity result. A separate quantum model could use related connectivity as couplings; it must define its own physics and measurements rather than inheriting the classical result’s interpretation.

## Assessment

1. Identify where the project moves from established mechanics into a proposal.
2. Define variables and units without metaphor substituting for equations.
3. Name at least one result that would contradict the specific hypothesis.
4. Identify specialist review and evidence needed before publication claims.

Deliver a two-page brief, source table and reproducible classical baseline. Use careful wording: “proposed model” or “analogy,” unless appropriate evidence supports a stronger statement. This module can help formulate a future paper; it does not validate prior Quantum Tensegrity claims or assert priority.
