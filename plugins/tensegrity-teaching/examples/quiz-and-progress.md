# Phase 01 learner quiz
1. Which force can an ideal flexible cable carry? A tension; B compression; C both equally. (1 point)
2. Which is the intended primary role of a strut? A carry compression; B supply electricity; C absorb all forces without deforming. (1 point)
3. A cable becomes slack. Which conclusion is justified? A the model is stronger; B that cable is not taut; C every member has failed. (1 point)
4. Does a floating-table appearance alone establish a strict tensegrity structure? Explain. (2 points)
5. Why should measured node-to-node span not automatically be used as cord cut length? (2 points)

# Instructor key
1. A. Cables pull; they cannot sustain compression like a rod. Remediation: observe a loose cord under a pushing attempt.
2. A. Compression members may buckle; no universal rigidity is implied. Remediation: label pushing forces in a sketch.
3. B. Do not infer overall stability from one visual sign. Remediation: inspect connectivity and joints with the model supported.
4. No (1); connectivity and force roles need examination, including a continuous tension network and disconnected compression members under the chosen definition (1). Remediation: compare labelled structures.
5. Node span omits joint/loop/knot allowances (1); stretch, rest length or real attachment offsets affect the required physical length (1). Remediation: measure a sample joint. Total 7 points.
