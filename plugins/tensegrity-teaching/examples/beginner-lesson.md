# First lesson: pulling, pushing and balance
Audience: adult beginners; 60 minutes; English; facilitator-prepared tabletop model. No learner assembly required.
Objectives: distinguish pulling from pushing; identify a cable and strut on a labelled model; explain why shape alone does not prove tensegrity.
Materials: loose cord, blunt craft rod, labelled demonstration model or a diagram, paper and marker. Inspect model joints first; if no controlled model is available, use the diagram only.

| Minutes | Learner action | Facilitator prompt | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Choose handling or observation and predict force roles | What happens if we pull a loose cord? | Prediction |
| 5–15 | Observe gentle cord pulling and rod pushing between hands | Can the loose cord push? | Correct pulling/pushing labels |
| 15–25 | Label cables, rods and attachment nodes | Which members form a continuous tension network? | Annotated diagram |
| 25–40 | Observe the prepared model and compare with a floating-table picture | Are compression members joined to one another? | Reasoned topology comparison |
| 40–50 | Sketch a model and describe one uncertainty | What could a photograph fail to show? | Distinguish observation from inference |
| 50–60 | Complete exit ticket and discuss next step | Explain your answer using a labelled member | Three answers |

Learner cards: 1. Choose a material or diagram to observe. 2. Label one pulling member. 3. Label one pushing member. 4. Circle a node. 5. Explain one limit of the picture.
Exit ticket: What force can a cord carry? What force is the strut intended to carry? Does a floating appearance prove strict tensegrity?
Instructor key: tension; compression; no—check member connectivity and prestress evidence. Next session: compare prestress and slack using observations, then prepare a supported pilot build.
Chinese opening: 今天我们先观察拉力和压力。你可以选择动手、看示范，或在图上标记。先作预测，再用观察说明理由。
