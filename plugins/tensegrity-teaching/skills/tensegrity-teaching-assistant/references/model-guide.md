# Tabletop three-strut pilot
Source: https://github.com/freedomyamato/tensegrity-from-scratch/blob/main/app/resources/three-strut-prism.md
Read on 2026-10-07 Singapore time. Source describes numerically verified ideal geometry but an untested physical workshop procedure. Preserve that distinction.

## Connectivity
Six nodes: bottom 0,1,2; top 3,4,5. Three disconnected compression struts and nine tension cables. Ideal radius 80 mm; height 140 mm; top rotated +30 degrees counterclockwise in the source x–y convention.

| Family | Endpoint pairs | Ideal node spans |
|---|---|---|
| Bottom cables | 0–1, 1–2, 2–0 | 138.564 mm |
| Top cables | 3–4, 4–5, 5–3 | 138.564 mm |
| Cross cables | 0–3, 1–4, 2–5 | 145.996 mm |
| Struts | 0–4, 1–5, 2–3 | 208.531 mm |

These are spans, not cord or rod cut lengths. Joint offsets, loops, knots and material stretch change the physical lengths. Use a consistent scale if available rods differ. Retrieve the coordinate and member schedules before detailed fabrication. Never derive a material load rating from these dimensions.

## Preparation and assembly
Use blunt lightweight rods, adjustable low-tension cords, labels, ruler, tray and cardboard supports. Prepare and gently test one sample joint with witness marks. Reject slipping or damaged joints. Adult/facilitator preparation of cuts is preferable for beginners.
1. Label the six nodes and verify all twelve endpoint pairs.
2. Hold the top and bottom positions in a jig; place the three struts at their scheduled endpoints, without joining rods along their lengths.
3. Attach bottom cables loosely; check labels.
4. Attach top cables loosely; check top-node order and twist.
5. Attach the three cross cables, remaining adjustable.
6. Snug gently in alternating small increments, watching joint marks and rod contact. Keep the face away from stretched components.
7. Reduce support gradually only when joints remain controlled. Resupport immediately on collapse or release.
8. Record spans, height, joint details and deviations. Do not add a panel or load in the first session.

## Troubleshooting sequence
Support the model → reduce tension gently → compare labelled endpoints with the schedule → inspect joint slip → check node geometry → adjust one cause → repeat observation.
- Collapse: first inspect missing cross cables, wrong labels and slipping joints.
- Rod contact: inspect twist, connectivity and real joint offsets; do not force separation.
- Slack: inspect rest length, knot take-up and geometry; avoid globally overtightening.
- Drift: record time and witness marks; consider joint slip, creep and method changes as hypotheses.
Stop on fraying, rod damage, sudden slip or uncontrolled movement. Do not perform destructive tests or advise hanging over people. A photograph cannot establish internal forces or safe capacity.
