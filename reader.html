Lesson 01: Goals and placement

Course contents · Phase 00: Orientation and workshop setup

Objective

Choose a learning route and identify what you can already demonstrate.

Prerequisite: None. Bring a learning goal and a way to record observations..

Planning time: approximately 90 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

Concept

A goal names an observable result. “Understand tensegrity” is broad; “assemble a labeled prism and explain its load paths” can be checked. Prior experience matters only when it transfers to this task. Mathematics, making and teaching are separate skills, so placement should not be based on one total score.

Worked example

A learner can assemble a kit but cannot distinguish mass in kg from force in N. They may use making experience while still completing lessons 13–16. For Ivan, the default route ends in a tabletop solar sculpture and reproducible workshop.

02 Materials and tools

Lesson 02: Materials and tools

Course contents · Phase 00: Orientation and workshop setup

Objective

Prepare a small kit and distinguish geometric dimensions from connection allowances.

Prerequisite: Lesson 01; or demonstrate its evidence check.

Planning time: approximately 90 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

Concept

A model includes members and joints. An ideal node is a point; a real knot, loop or cap occupies space and may slip. Record both the distance between node centers and the total cut length. Start with blunt, lightweight struts, cord and adjustable loops; use elastic only as a clearly documented classroom substitution.

Worked example

A target cable has a 140 mm node-to-node span. Two 15 mm knot allowances suggest an initial 170 mm cut, but actual knot take-up must be checked on a sample. Never treat 170 mm as the structural member length.

Materials and setup

Blunt craft struts or a classroom kit, cord, ruler, reusable clips, tape, tray and optional pre-cut loops. Cutting is performed by a competent adult when needed.

  4. Measure its node span, overall length and repeatability.

03 Observation and measurement

Lesson 03: Observation and measurement

Course contents · Phase 00: Orientation and workshop setup

Objective

Measure repeatedly and state resolution without inventing precision.

Prerequisite: Lesson 02; or demonstrate its evidence check.

Planning time: approximately 90 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

Concept

Resolution is the smallest readable increment. Repeatability describes how readings vary when the measurement is repeated. Neither guarantees accuracy: a misaligned ruler can produce repeatable but biased readings. Label the endpoints, units and measuring method so somebody else can reproduce your result.

Worked example

Three readings are 139, 140 and 139 mm. Their mean is 139.33 mm, but a 1 mm ruler does not justify reporting the span as exactly 139.333333 mm. Report the raw readings and approximately 139 mm with the instrument resolution.

Materials and setup

Ruler with known markings, cord sample, paper and stable flat surface.

Use a private copy of the experiment record. Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

  1. Define the two measurement endpoints with tape marks.
  2. Read the span without pulling the cord harder each time.
  3. Remove and replace the ruler for three readings.
  4. Calculate mean and range: maximum minus minimum.
  5. Exchange instructions with another person or remeasure after a break; compare.

Expected behavior and interpretation

Small variation is visible in the raw record. Differences prompt a method check before a design change.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

Troubleshooting and limits

Looking diagonally causes parallax. Changing tension changes the specimen rather than just the reading.

05 Tension versus compression

Lesson 05: Tension versus compression

Course contents · Phase 01: Structural intuition

Objective

Identify what a flexible cable and a strut can carry.

Prerequisite: Lesson 04; or demonstrate its evidence check.

Planning time: approximately 180 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

Concept

Tension pulls along a member; compression pushes along it. A flexible cord cannot reliably resist compression: it becomes slack or bends. A strut can carry compression but may buckle. In our numerical convention positive force means tension and negative force means compression; the sign must be stated rather than assumed.

Worked example

A 2 N tensile cable pulls each attached node toward the other. The two end forces are equal and opposite. A compressed strut pushes its nodes away from each other. The net force on the whole unloaded member can be zero while internal force is nonzero.

Materials and setup

String, soft sponge, blunt craft stick, pencil and paper.

Use a private copy of the experiment record. Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

  1. Gently pull the string and draw forces at its two ends.
  2. Move the ends together and observe slack.
  3. Gently compress a sponge to represent compression.
  4. Draw end forces on a strut without bending it to failure.
  5. Label three components in a reference prism as tension or compression members.

Expected behavior and interpretation

The learner sees that a balance of external forces does not mean the member is unstressed.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

Troubleshooting and limits

Do not confuse force sign with spatial direction. Negative compression is a convention, not a downward force.

06 Load paths

Lesson 06: Load paths

Course contents · Phase 01: Structural intuition

Objective

Trace a load through connections to external supports.

Prerequisite: Lesson 05; or demonstrate its evidence check.

Planning time: approximately 180 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

Concept

A load path follows how forces transfer between members and eventually to supports. Internal forces occur in action–reaction pairs; reactions from the table or anchors are external to the structure. A complete diagram must say which object is being isolated. A free prism with only self-stress is a different problem from a prism resting on a table under gravity.

Worked example

For a tabletop model with total mass 0.2 kg, its weight is approximately 1.962 N using g=9.81 m/s². In static vertical balance the sum of upward table reactions is 1.962 N. Equal individual reactions require additional symmetry assumptions.

Materials and setup

A model or the supplied prism drawing; paper and colored pencils.

Use a private copy of the experiment record. Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

  1. Draw a boundary around the entire model.
  2. Mark gravity and table contact forces outside that boundary.
  3. Choose one top node and draw its connected members.
  4. Trace the possible force routes to bottom contacts.
  5. Explain why the free self-stress model has no table reactions but the physical model does.

Expected behavior and interpretation

The whole-structure diagram includes external forces while the node diagram contains member forces.

If your result differs, keep it. Recheck units, endpoint definitions, instruments and assumptions before changing the design. A mismatch can be the most useful evidence.

Troubleshooting and limits

Do not distribute weight equally without checking symmetry, contact and center of mass.

07 Prestress and slack

Lesson 07: Prestress and slack

Course contents · Phase 01: Structural intuition

Objective

Explain internal force before external loading and detect slack.

Prerequisite: Lesson 06; or demonstrate its evidence check.

Planning time: approximately 180 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

Concept

Prestress is an initial internal force state. In a free ideal tensegrity, self-stress can balance at every node without external loading. Cables must remain tensile in the intended state. Prestress may alter stiffness, but increasing it also raises joint demand and strut compression. Equilibrium, stability and material capacity are different checks.

Worked example

For a unilateral spring with stiffness 100 N/m, rest length 0.14 m and current length 0.15 m, tension is 1 N. At 0.13 m the model returns 0 N, not −1 N, because a cable does not carry compression.

Materials and setup

Cord or low-tension elastic sample, ruler; optional Python 3.10 or newer.

Use a private copy of the experiment record. Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

  1. Observe a cord at three gentle extensions without releasing it suddenly.
  2. Record when it is slack and when it is taut.
  3. Calculate the two example spring forces.
  4. If using Python, run the spring lab at lengths 0.15 and 0.13 m.
  5. Explain why a spring estimate is not proof of prism stability.

09 Nodes and connectivity

Lesson 09: Nodes and connectivity

Course contents · Phase 02: Geometry and making

Objective

Translate the prism into a node and member schedule.

Prerequisite: Lesson 08; or demonstrate its evidence check.

Planning time: approximately 270 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

Concept

Topology says which nodes are connected; geometry says where they are. Our prism uses bottom nodes 0,1,2 and top nodes 3,4,5. B and T are triangle-edge cables; C joins corresponding bottom/top nodes; S joins bottom i to top (i+1) modulo 3. The six nodes and twelve members must be labeled consistently across drawings and code.

Worked example

C0 connects 0–3. S0 connects 0–4, S1 connects 1–5 and S2 connects 2–3. At node 0, B0, B2, C0 and S0 meet. A missing C0 changes topology even if the model still looks similar.

Materials and setup

Use a private copy of the experiment record. Assign a specimen or activity ID and record which values are measured, calculated or illustrative.

  2. Copy all twelve member endpoint pairs.
  3. List the four members meeting node 0.
  4. Count how many times each member endpoint appears across nodes.
  5. Match the drawing labels to the supplied digital prism output.

11 Twist, symmetry and cable length

Lesson 11: Twist, symmetry and cable length

Course contents · Phase 02: Geometry and making

Objective

Relate rotation to member lengths without assuming all twists are equilibria.

Prerequisite: Lesson 10; or demonstrate its evidence check.

Planning time: approximately 270 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

Concept

At fixed radius R and height h, the corresponding-node cable length is sqrt(h²+2R²(1−cos α)). Our offset strut length is sqrt(h²+2R²(1−cos(α+120°))). Symmetry makes each family equal in length. Changing α changes geometry; it need not preserve an admissible self-stress for the same connectivity.

Worked example

For R=0.08 m and h=0.14 m, at α=0° each corresponding-node cable is 0.14 m. At α=30° it is approximately 0.14600 m. The 30° example has a valid analytic self-stress; the twist sweep checks other angles separately.

  3. Generate prism outputs at 30° and 45° using different output filenames.
  4. Compare member lengths and nullspace dimensions.
  5. Separate geometric feasibility from force-equilibrium feasibility in your notes.

Lesson 12: Repeatable assembly and joints

47 Results and limitations

Lesson 47: Results and limitations

Course contents · Phase 11: Capstone and research judgment

Objective

Summarize evidence without overstating precision or generality.

Prerequisite: Lesson 46; or demonstrate its evidence check.

Planning time: approximately 240 minutes including practice and recording. Split into short sessions as needed.

Concept

Report raw values, a summary and the boundary of your claim. A mean alone hides spread. Explain confounders, stopped trials and measurement limits. Separate numerical verification from physical validation. For an exploratory hypothesis, specify how future observations might falsify it rather than interpreting every result as support.

Worked example

Synthetic angle errors A=(1,2,3)° and B=(1,1,2)° have means 2° and 1.33° with ranges 2° and 1°. The sample suggests B is more repeatable here, but three trials and instrument resolution do not establish a universal structural advantage.

Materials and setup

Trial records, calculator and templates/capstone-report.md.

Predict and practice

Before acting, write the result you expect and one observation that could disagree with it.

  1. Present raw values including stopped or missing trials.
  2. Calculate mean and range for each comparable metric.
  3. Describe instrument resolution and uncontrolled differences.
  4. State a conclusion restricted to tested specimens and conditions.
  5. Name one next experiment that would reduce an important uncertainty.

The report supports a narrow conclusion and an explicit next step.
