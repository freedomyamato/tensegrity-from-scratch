# Facilitator handbook

## Prepare the group

Choose a single objective per session and inspect all classroom components. Preview the sequence and completed example. Let participants choose suitable response forms, pace and supports. Use labels and shapes in addition to color; provide pre-cut components or a ready-made model when making demands are unrelated to the objective.

For sessions involving autistic learners, select adaptations with the learner and relevant educators/support staff. A diagnosis does not specify one uniform profile. This handbook proposes education supports, not treatment or guaranteed outcomes. Institutional implementation should be co-designed rather than assumed.

## Before every physical session

Check blunt ends, secure low-tension joints and a contained tabletop. Set stop conditions for slip, fraying, damaged struts or unexpected release. Avoid large suspended weights, high-energy elastics, motorized actuation, roofs and water. Substituting a drawing or digital task is valid when physical handling is unsuitable.

## Teach a lesson

1. State the objective and show the intended evidence.
2. Invite a prediction using words, a drawing or pointing.
3. Explain the concept with the lesson’s worked example. Define units and symbols.
4. Run the activity in manageable steps. Pause at checks, not only at failures.
5. Compare results with prediction. Keep disagreement rather than hiding it.
6. Ask the teach-back quiz, then consult the phase answer key.
7. Apply the rubric to the objective and record the next action.

If a learner completes assembly but cannot explain forces, retain the assembly success and revisit force intuition. If a learner explains forces but cannot tie knots, offer a preassembled model and assess understanding separately.

## Observe without overclaiming

Record what was done, which support was used and what remained unclear. Avoid diagnostic interpretations. Request consent before any image/video recording and keep identifiable records in private storage. Public course examples should be anonymized or synthetic and labeled accordingly.

Pilot the timing with a small group. Keep feedback about instructions, access, pacing and materials. Stop or redesign a task when its demands prevent the objective from being taught effectively. This release’s timing estimates and adaptations have not been classroom validated.

## Workforce skills

Use sorting, component identification, measuring to a written criterion, assembly checks and inspection logging. Give constructive feedback connected to specific evidence. These activities build a practice portfolio; they do not promise work placement or confer accredited technical qualification.

## Useful questions

- “Which node is being isolated?”
- “What was measured, and what was assumed?”
- “Where does the load go?”
- “What could explain the disagreement?”
- “What would someone else need to reproduce this?”
